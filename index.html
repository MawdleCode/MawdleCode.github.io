TwinThrust

One thumb per engine. Every landing is earned.

A 2D physics rocket lander for Android. Free.

Read the devlog →

Two engines. Two thumbs.

Most landers give you one throttle. TwinThrust gives you two — the left thumb drives the left engine, the right thumb the right one, and nothing sits between you and gravity.

Feather one engine to lean into a turn. Slam both to kill a freefall. Hold a hover with two uneven thumbs and you are doing real physics, not pressing a button. It takes about ten seconds to understand and a lot longer than that to be good at.

There is no autopilot, no assist, and no procedural filler. Every one of the 40 levels was built by hand around exactly one new idea.

Forty levels, three chapters, one idea each

From a first moonlit hop, out through laser gates and meteor showers and a gravity well, into a burnt machine-world — and then into a forest that is aware you are flying through it.

Energy timers

No lives, no waits, no stamina bar. Short sessions, instant restarts, and your own best time to beat.

Built solo, in Godot 4

One developer, no engine licence, and a test rig that flies the levels by itself.

The levels are verified by booting the real game and having an autopilot fly the craft headless — which is the only reliable way to catch an invisible wall that no amount of looking at the scene will show you.

The devlog is where the things that went wrong get written up properly: the one-line physics bug that cost three weeks, the shader that was fine on one phone and blocky on another, and the test rig that spent a week guarding a line of dialogue that had already been deleted.
