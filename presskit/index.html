One line

A physics rocket lander where each engine has its own thumb — 40 hand-crafted levels where every landing is earned.

Three sentences

TwinThrust is a 2D physics rocket game with one twist: the left thumb throttles the left engine, the right thumb the right engine, and nothing else sits between you and gravity. Forty hand-built levels across three chapters take the craft from a first moonlit hop through laser gates, gravity wells and ion storms, into a burnt machine-world and then a living forest that watches you fly. It is the classic lunar-lander feel rebuilt for touch — easy to grasp, brutal to master, and honest about it.

One paragraph

TwinThrust is a solo-developed 2D physics rocket lander for Android, built in Godot 4. Instead of a single throttle, each engine is controlled independently — feather one to lean into a turn, slam both to kill a freefall. There is no procedural filler: each of the 40 levels is built by hand around exactly one new idea, from threading counter-rotating asteroid rings and flying blind through blackout corridors, to Chapter 2's burnt machine-world Ashmoor and Chapter 3's Verdance, a living forest that closes with a finale at the World Tree. Three difficulty ranks — Cadet, Wingman and Pilot — put a star on each level for the rank you clear it at. Sessions are short, there are no energy timers, and personal-best times keep the "one more try" loop honest.

Key features

  • Two engines, two thumbs, full independent throttle control
  • 40 hand-crafted levels across 3 themed chapters; every level introduces one new mechanic
  • Three difficulty ranks with a per-level star for the rank you clear it at
  • A Rocket Bay with 7 rocket models × 12 colour schemes × 5 engine flame styles
  • Synthesised engine audio that reacts to your throttle
  • Level timers and personal bests; no energy systems, no forced waits

Story hooks for coverage

  • The two-thumb throttle — genuinely different from every other lunar-lander descendant, and the whole design follows from it.
  • The levels are flown by a robot before they are flown by a person. Level changes are verified by booting the real game headless and having an autopilot fly the craft — it catches invisible walls and collider bugs that inspecting the scene cannot.
  • One new idea per level, enforced. A self-imposed rule that shapes the whole game: if a level does not introduce something, it does not ship.
  • Chapter 3's forest is a character. The environment reacts to the player rather than decorating them.
