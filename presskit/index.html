One line

A physics rocket lander where each engine has its own thumb, across 40 hand-crafted levels where every landing is earned.

Three sentences

TwinThrust is a 2D physics rocket game with one twist: the left thumb throttles the left engine, the right thumb throttles the right engine, and nothing else sits between the player and gravity. Forty hand-built levels across three chapters run from open space, through the firelit keeps and torch-lit dungeons of Ashmoor, into the Verdance, a rain-soaked jungle where the weather itself drains your fuel. It is the classic lunar-lander feel rebuilt for touch: easy to grasp, hard to master, and honest about which of the two it is being.

One paragraph

TwinThrust is a solo-developed 2D physics rocket lander for Android, built in Godot 4. Instead of a single throttle, each engine is controlled independently, so the player feathers one to lean into a turn and slams both to kill a freefall. There is no procedural filler: each of the 40 levels is built by hand around exactly one new idea, from threading counter-rotating asteroid rings and escaping a gravity well in Chapter 1, to flying a pitch-dark dungeon by headlight past swinging maces in Chapter 2's medieval realm of Ashmoor, to Chapter 3's Verdance, where constant rain leaks the fuel tank and the route through a level is also a budget. Four difficulty ranks run from Cadet, which steadies the craft and halves hazard speed, to Ace, which tightens every margin and unlocks only once the game is finished, and each level remembers the highest rank it has been cleared at.

Key features

  • Two engines, two thumbs, full independent throttle control
  • 40 hand-crafted levels across 3 themed chapters, each level built around one new mechanic
  • Four difficulty ranks (Cadet, Wingman, Pilot, Ace) with a per-level star for the highest rank cleared
  • A Rocket Bay with 7 rocket models, 12 colour schemes and 5 engine flame styles
  • Limited fuel as Chapter 3's signature constraint, drained by the rain and refilled by sap pods
  • Headlight flying through blackout dungeons and caves
  • Synthesised engine audio that reacts to throttle
  • Level timers and personal bests

Story hooks for coverage

  • The two-thumb throttle is genuinely different from every other lunar-lander descendant, and the whole design follows from it.
  • The levels are flown by a robot before they are flown by a person. Level changes are verified by booting the real game headless and having an autopilot fly the craft, which catches invisible walls and collider bugs that inspecting the scene cannot.
  • One new idea per level, enforced. A self-imposed rule that shapes the whole game: if a level does not introduce something, it does not ship.
  • Three chapters that look nothing alike. Open space, then a firelit medieval realm, then a rain-soaked jungle, each with its own hazards and its own palette.
  • The Verdance makes weather a resource. Rain drains the tank whether you are burning or not, so hovering to think costs exactly as much as flying.
